Morning Meeting
Resource Pack

10 ready-to-use activities — greetings, sharing prompts, group activities, and morning messages — to start any school day with connection and calm. No prep required.

The Four Parts of a Morning Meeting
1

Greeting

Every student is welcomed by name. A predictable, friendly start that tells each child: you belong here today.

2

Sharing

Students share news or respond to a prompt while classmates practice listening and asking follow-up questions.

3

Group Activity

A short, whole-class activity that builds cooperation and gives everyone a reason to laugh together.

4

Morning Message

A brief written message or puzzle that bridges the meeting into the learning day ahead.

How to use this pack

Set aside 10–15 minutes at the start of the day. Pick one activity from each part for a full meeting, or use any single activity as a quick community builder. Every activity works with zero materials unless noted — just you, your students, and a circle.

10 Ready-to-Use Activities
of·the·day
GreetingK–8 · 5–8 min

Name + Gesture Greeting

Each student says their name and invents a unique gesture. The class mirrors it back.

  1. Gather in a circle and model it yourself first — say your name and add a gesture.
  2. The whole class echoes your name and mirrors your gesture.
  3. Continue around the circle so every student gets their moment.
Teacher tip: For older grades, turn it into a "gesture chain" — each student repeats the previous two gestures before adding their own.
GreetingK–8 · 3–5 min

Partner Greeting Remix

Greet a partner by name, then add one kind sentence or question before switching.

  1. Pair students up. Each greets the other by name: "Good morning, ___!"
  2. Each partner adds one kind sentence or a genuine question.
  3. Rotate partners two or three times so students greet someone new.
Teacher tip: Post sentence starters ("I hope you…", "Did you…?") for students who freeze up.
SharingK–8 · 5–10 min

Weekend Highlight Share

Share one moment from the weekend using the sentence starter: "One thing I did was…"

  1. Display the sentence starter and give 30 seconds of quiet think time.
  2. Students share in pairs, or go around the circle.
  3. Invite one or two follow-up questions from listeners.
Teacher tip: Always allow a "pass." Inviting — never forcing — keeps sharing feeling safe.
Sharing2–8 · 8–10 min

Two Truths and a Wish

Share two true things about yourself and one thing you wish were true. Class guesses the wish.

  1. Model your own two truths and a wish first.
  2. Students jot down their three statements.
  3. Volunteers share; classmates vote on which one is the wish.
Teacher tip: Ask guessers "What made you think that?" — it quietly builds evidence-based reasoning.
Group ActivityK–8 · 5–8 min

Commonality Circle

Find one thing all students in a small group have in common. Groups share with the class.

  1. Split into groups of four or five.
  2. Give two minutes to find something everyone in the group shares — beyond the obvious.
  3. Each group announces its commonality to the class.
Teacher tip: For round two, ban anything visible ("we all wear sneakers") to push toward foods, places, and favorites.
Group Activity1–8 · 5 min

Collaborative Counting

The class counts to 20 together — but no two people can speak at the same time. Start over on overlap.

  1. No order, no signals — anyone may say the next number.
  2. If two voices overlap, the count restarts at one.
  3. Celebrate loudly when the class reaches 20.
Teacher tip: Too easy? Raise the target to 30, or play with eyes closed.

Morning MessageK–8 · 3–5 min

Riddle of the Day

Display a grade-appropriate riddle. Students think quietly, then share guesses.

  1. Post the riddle before students arrive.
  2. Students think silently as they settle in.
  3. Collect guesses, then reveal the answer with a drumroll.
Teacher tip: Keep a running tally of "class solves" — streaks make it a morning ritual.
Morning Message2–8 · 3–5 min

Connection Question

Post a question on the board. Students write a one-sentence answer before the meeting begins.

  1. Post a question tied to today's learning or your classroom community.
  2. Students answer in one sentence — sticky note or journal.
  3. Read a few aloud (with permission) to open the meeting.
Teacher tip: Save the strongest answers — they make great discussion prompts later in the week.
SEL PromptK–8 · 3–5 min

Emoji Check-In

Each student chooses an emoji that matches their energy this morning and briefly explains why.

  1. Display six to eight emoji options on the board.
  2. Each student picks one and explains in a single sentence.
  3. Quietly follow up later with students who chose low-energy emojis.
Teacher tip: Chart the class mood across a week, then discuss the patterns together on Friday.
Brain TeaserK–8 · 5 min

What Comes Next?

Show a visual or number pattern. Students identify the rule and predict what comes next.

  1. Display a pattern — shapes, numbers, or colors.
  2. Independent think time, then pair-share the rule.
  3. The class predicts the next item and tests the prediction.
Teacher tip: Hand it over — let a student design tomorrow's pattern.
