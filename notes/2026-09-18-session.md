## Finding: two duplicate live subscriptions charged $158 to the owner's card

During the 2026-08-25 Stripe go-live testing, **two** annual subscriptions were
created 60 seconds apart on the same customer — `cus_V8iApOF9EQQ61Q` /
`dembryllc@gmail.com`:

| Subscription | Created (UTC) | Trial ended | Invoice | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sub_1U8Qk1B2eRKsbhTpW6YWccxr` | 2026-08-25 20:14 | 2026-09-08 | `UI1IWEOS-0003` — $79 paid | active, renews 2027-09-08 |
| `sub_1U8QklB2eRKsbhTptUiWmHFz` | 2026-08-25 20:15 | 2026-09-08 | `UI1IWEOS-0004` — $79 paid | active, renews 2027-09-08 |

The 14-day trials (`trial_period_days: 14`, $0 due at checkout) both converted
on 2026-09-08 and charged $79 each. The *monthly* test subscription from the
same night (`sub_1U8W0GB2eRKsbhTplGiAgH0l`, `cus_V8il4Ssd1PV7DO`) was cancelled
cleanly inside its trial for $0 — it is the annual pair that was missed.

These three are the only OfTheDay subscriptions that have ever existed on the
live account, so no real customer revenue is mixed in — the full $158 is the
owner's own card. Stripe generally does not return processing fees on refunds,
so roughly $5 of it does not come back regardless.

**Not actioned this session** — cancelling live subscriptions and refunding real
charges needs an explicit go-ahead. When it happens, it doubles as the first
production exercise of the `customer.subscription.deleted` path, which has never
fired live.

**Lesson for future live testing:** use one subscription, cancel it the same
session, and write the subscription ID into the session note so a forgotten
trial cannot silently convert three weeks later.
